ACADEMIC RIGOR

Six Measurable Research Objectives

Each objective addresses a core machine learning question and was validated on the California Housing dataset.

Obj 1

Data Audit & KNN Imputation

Audited 20,640 records. Exactly 207 rows missing total_bedrooms (1.0%). Compared Simple Median Imputer against KNN Imputer (k=5) inside cross-validation folds. KNN preserved multivariate covariance between rooms and bedrooms without test leakage.

207 Imputed records preserved
Obj 2

Income Non-Linearity & Price Caps

Median income is the single strongest predictor (r = 0.69). Target is right-censored at $500,001 (4.3% of test samples), which produces 16% of the overall squared error. Investigated polynomial and non-linear tree splits.

r = 0.69 Income-Price correlation
Obj 3

Geography & Spatial Autocorrelation

Spatial autocorrelation of house values is severe (Moran's I = 0.79). Engineered public geographic hub distance features to 5 key California cities (SF, LA, SD, SJ, Sacramento) plus coastal proximity, reducing spatial residuals.

I = 0.79 Moran's spatial clustering index
Obj 4

Ratio Feature Engineering

Raw room and bedroom aggregates scale with population size. Engineered compound ratios: rooms_per_household, bedrooms_ratio (bedrooms/rooms), and population_per_household. Features ranked top 5 in permutation importance.

+$1,500 RMSE improvement from ratios
Obj 5

Multi-Model Hyperparameter Tuning

Evaluated 7 distinct models from simple baselines to tree ensembles. Tuned with 3-fold randomized search, validated with 5-fold CV. XGBoost with histogram binning outperformed standard Random Forest and HistGB across all metrics.

7 Models Systematically tuned & compared
Obj 6

Error Diagnostics & SHAP Explainability

Residual analysis revealed heteroskedasticity (larger variance on expensive houses). Computed TreeSHAP values for global and local interpretability, proving income and distance to San Francisco are dominant valuation drivers.

TreeSHAP Exact local feature attribution
